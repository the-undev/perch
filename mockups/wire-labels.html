The wire's label

A wire's name and tally sit small above its left end, and since the label inset the birds start past the text, so a longer tally pushes every bird further right. This round separates the label's two jobs. The name is identity: it can be large and quiet, painted on the sky behind the birds, where nothing needs to give way to it. The tally is status: it stays small, bright and in front, in a room of its own whose width never depends on the text.

Option 1 · The painted name (recommended)

The name is painted on the sky: the display face, sentence case, about 52 units tall at 12% ink, baseline resting just above the wire's left end, drawn behind the wire and every bird. Birds cross it freely and always win; in front of it everything stays crisp. The tally hangs under the wire's left end, one word per line, full colour. The birds start at a fixed 240 units, one bird's room, whatever the text says: a longer name just paints further behind the flock, and a longer tally wraps in its column.

The paint and the plumage

At 12% the paint dries to almost exactly the plumage colour, so a crossing bird melts into the letters. Two cures, beside the problem: brighter paint at 18%, the bird reading as a dark silhouette against lighter letters; or the cut, the paint kept at 12% but trimmed back around each bird by a thin rim of sky, so the bird keeps its outline and the letters stay quiet. The cut is the recommendation.

Option 2 · All scenery

The whole label is painted behind: the name as in option 1, and the tally bigger than today, 15 units and full colour, on the old line above the wire's left end, also behind the birds. No inset at all: the birds start at the margin. The cost is honest occlusion: a bird that lands on the tally hides part of a count.
