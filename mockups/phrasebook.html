Perch Phrasebook

What a bird says in every situation the derivations can produce. The speech carries how the bird feels and why; the chips carry the numbers it holds. Each entry names its trigger; the rules below govern anything not listed.

The voice

  • First person, present tense, three sentences at most.
  • The worst input speaks first; at most one secondary follows; everything else stays in the chips.
  • Numbers name quantity and time plainly; judgement lives in tone, never in adjectives about the owner.
  • Tone follows mood: content birds are brief, restless birds nudge, distressed birds are blunt but never hysterical.
  • No suggestions, with one exception: a bird may echo the owner's own config expectations back ("you said I should have CI").
  • At night every speech gains the waking prefix.
  • Calm punctuation: no exclamation marks, ever.
